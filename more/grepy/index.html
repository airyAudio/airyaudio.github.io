Why Grepy

grep, with a window around it.

Everything you'd do with grep, zgrep and a few pipes, in one Mac app, with a tree of what you search and a viewer for what you find.

Archives inside archives

zip, tar, tgz, gz, xz, bz2, zst, 7z, rar and more, opened one inside another as deep as they go. Each match shows its full path, through every archive.

Regex or text

A regular expression or plain text, match case (Aa) and whole words, lines of context before and after, and include and exclude masks for file names.

Filter by time

Keep only the lines from the minutes that matter. Grepy reads the usual log times: ISO, 31/10/2026, Oct 31, Apache, epoch and time only.

A tree of your files

The sidebar shows every folder, file and archive. Tick what to search, and filter the tree by file name.

More filters

Add filters with AND and OR, or NOT to drop the lines you don't want. Open a file in the viewer, with every match highlighted.

Private and sandboxed

Grepy reads only what you choose and changes nothing. Archives are opened with macOS's own libarchive, inside the App Sandbox, and no data leaves your Mac.

Frequently asked questions

When will Grepy be available?

Soon, on the Mac App Store. This page will have the link as soon as it's there.

Which Macs will it run on?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Does Grepy change my files or archives?

No. It only reads them. Archives are unpacked into Grepy's own temporary folder, so a second search doesn't unpack them again. The space they take shows as Disk used, and they are deleted when you press Clear and when you quit.

Which archive formats does it open?

zip, tar, tgz, gz, xz, bz2, zst, 7z, rar and the other formats macOS's libarchive reads, also one inside another.

Are there keyboard shortcuts?

⌘↵ starts the search and ⌘. cancels it. You can also drag folders, files and archives onto the window.

Does Grepy collect any data?

No. See the Privacy Policy.
